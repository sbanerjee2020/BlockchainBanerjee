01

Perpetuals, plainly

A perpetual future ("perp") is the single instrument behind most of the leverage in crypto. Before looking at Hyperliquid or any US venue, get this mechanism solid: it is the same math everywhere it shows up.

QWhat is perpetual futures trading, and why is it such a big deal?

A regular futures contract locks in a price for an asset on a set expiry date, like a forward-dated invoice. A perpetual future has no expiry. You can hold it for an hour or three years. It behaves like leveraged spot trading, except you never actually own the coin, stock, or barrel of oil: you own a contract whose value tracks it.

The trick is keeping a contract with no expiry date glued to the real ("spot") price of the asset, since there is no expiry date to force convergence. Exchanges solve this with the funding rate: a small periodic payment between longs (buyers) and shorts (sellers). [1]

Why does this matter so much? Three reasons:

  • Leverage without borrowing. Post $1,000 as margin, control a $10,000 position. There is no loan paperwork and no collateral custody at a lender; the exchange nets your margin against your open risk in real time.
  • It is the dominant way the world trades crypto. Perpetuals account for the large majority of crypto derivatives volume, because they never force you to "roll" a position at expiry.
  • It went from crypto curiosity to systemic financial story in 2026. Regulators, CME, Kraken, Coinbase, Robinhood, Kalshi, wallets like MetaMask and Phantom, and Hyperliquid are now competing to host this flow, and it is spreading from crypto into stocks, oil, gold and indices.

Worked example: a $1,000 BTC perp trade

BTC spot is $110,000. You open a long with $1,000 margin at 10x leverage.

$10,000Notional position size (margin x leverage)
0.0909 BTCContract exposure ($10,000 / $110,000)
about 10%Adverse move that wipes out the margin (varies by venue)

BTC rises 5% to $115,500 and your $10,000 notional gains $500. Against your $1,000 margin that is a +50% return. Leverage cuts both ways: a 5% move against you erases half your margin, and a ~10% adverse move at 10x gets you close to liquidation, where the exchange force-closes the position before losses exceed your margin. The numbers are illustrative.

QWhy are perps suddenly so interesting?

Three forces converged in 2026:

  1. Scale, and a shift on-chain. CoinGecko's 2026 perpetuals report puts 2025 perp volume at $85.3 trillion on centralized exchanges and $6.38 trillion on-chain, roughly four times the on-chain figure for 2024. On-chain volume rose from about 3% to a 13% peak of centralized volume during 2025 (10% in April 2026), and DefiLlama shows on-chain perp venues at about $614 billion over the 30 days to October 3, 2026. [2][3]
  2. The US spent years locked out. Because the CFTC treats leveraged, margined, no-delivery crypto contracts as regulated derivatives, the big offshore perp venues (Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid) geo-block US persons. An enormous, liquid market grew up offshore, out of reach of US retail.
  3. That wall started coming down. A joint SEC/CFTC digital-asset taxonomy (March 17, 2026) clarified the CFTC's jurisdiction, and regulated US perps arrived: Coinbase (July 2025), Kraken (June 2026) and, announced on September 29, 2026, Robinhood. [4][5][6]

Hyperliquid

The platform most responsible for turning perpetuals from a crypto-native niche into a mainstream financial story in 2026: how it began, why it leads, how its token economics work, who is wrapping it, and what could go wrong.

Updated October 3, 2026 with live data from DefiLlama, CoinGecko, SoSoValue and Hyperliquid's own docs.

QWhat is Hyperliquid?

Hyperliquid is its own purpose-built Layer-1 blockchain, designed to run a fully on-chain order book, the same style of matching engine a stock exchange or Binance uses, instead of the slower automated-market-maker pools most DeFi exchanges rely on. It has two halves:

  • HyperCore: the trading engine itself. The order book, matching, margining and liquidation logic run on-chain at speeds that feel like a centralized exchange.
  • HyperEVM: a Solidity-compatible smart-contract layer next to HyperCore, so standard Hardhat and Foundry tooling works. Contracts here can read live HyperCore data (balances, positions, oracle prices) and send trading actions. [20]

It is non-custodial (you connect a wallet; there is no account or KYC to trade), lists hundreds of perpetual markets, and has begun listing non-crypto instruments through its HIP-3 framework: stocks, commodities, indices and even an S&P 500 perpetual. Section 4 goes deep on how this works under the hood, and Section 5 on how to build on it.

QWhere did Hyperliquid come from?

The origin story matters for the investment case, because it explains the design choices: no venture capital, a tiny team, and a bias for transparent, on-chain accounting. Jeff Yan, a former Hudson River Trading quant, ran an anonymous trading firm, Chameleon, then built Hyperliquid after the FTX collapse. The core team is about 11 people and raised no outside capital. Yan's stated ambition: "I hope Hyperliquid becomes the internet for money." [26][28][27]

  1. 2020Chameleon Trading. Yan starts an anonymous market-making firm.
  2. 2022 to 2023FTX collapses. Yan shuts Chameleon and builds a Layer-1 chain in about three months. [26]
  3. May 2023HLP vault. The trading strategies go into an on-chain vault anyone can deposit into, with "no fees and no carry". It bootstraps liquidity without paying market makers. [28]
  4. June 2023Mainnet. The order book goes live on its own chain.
  5. Nov 29, 2024HYPE genesis. 310M HYPE (31% of supply) is airdropped to users, with no venture or investor allocation. [14]
  6. Feb 2025HyperEVM. Smart contracts arrive on the same chain.
  7. Oct 13, 2025HIP-3. Anyone who meets the stake can deploy perp markets.
  8. Mar 18, 2026S&P 500 perp. trade.xyz licenses the index from S&P Dow Jones Indices. [30]
  9. May 2026HIP-4 and ETFs. Outcome markets go live (May 2) and the first US spot HYPE ETFs list. [22]
  10. Sep 23, 2026Record high. HYPE reaches $97.96 (CoinGecko), up from about $63 in June. [28]

QWhen will Hyperliquid come onshore in the US?

No firm date exists. Hyperliquid's own app still blocks US IP addresses, and its terms treat US persons as restricted. What has changed is that a path is being drawn:

  • Aug 19: President Trump said CFTC Chair Mike Selig was working to bring Hyperliquid into the US "in a fully compliant fashion"; HYPE jumped 11%. [18]
  • Aug 31: Bloomberg reported Hyperliquid was in talks with Payward, Kraken's parent. [44]
  • Sep 16: Payward detailed a plan: Bitnomial would be the regulated exchange and clearinghouse, NinjaTrader Clearing would carry accounts, and matching would happen on Hyperliquid, starting with HIP-3 markets. It needs CFTC approval, has no launch date, and a former SEC counsel estimated 10 to 12 months at minimum. [44]
  • Sep 9: The Hyperliquid Policy Center backed the CFTC in CME's lawsuit. [19]
  • Right now: nothing about actual US market access has changed. This is a live negotiation, not a done deal.

QHow can a blockchain and AI team participate?

Split this into two lanes, because they carry very different compliance profiles for a US-based team:

Lane A: Build (open to a US team)

  1. Ship a front-end or bot with a builder code. Earn up to 0.1% of the perp orders you route, with no matching engine to build (Section 5).
  2. Ship a HyperEVM dApp. Standard Solidity tooling works; contracts can read HyperCore state. [20]
  3. Deploy a HIP-3 market for an asset class nobody serves, by staking 500,000 HYPE (about $45M at $89.87). It is capital-heavy and operationally demanding (Sections 4 and 5). [21]
  4. Build the AI layer. The API is built for algorithmic access: signal generation, risk monitoring and execution agents inside hard limits.
  5. Run a validator. Stake HYPE to help secure consensus: infrastructure, not trading.

Lane B: Trade (blocked for US persons)

Building tools, running a validator or writing bots against the API is different from trading as a US person on Hyperliquid's markets, which the platform restricts. Do not use a VPN to bypass the block, and get legal advice on whether your own product makes you a venue or broker.

QWhat is the significance of HYPE's buybacks and fees?

The mechanism institutions keep pointing to is the buyback engine: roughly 97% of trading fees flow to an Assistance Fund that buys HYPE on the open market and permanently removes it from circulation. About 49 million HYPE (4.9% of maximum supply) has been retired so far, and cumulative buybacks are around $1.3 billion. The more the platform is used, the smaller the supply gets. [23][24][33]

Worked example: buybacks versus a scheduled unlock

In Q2 2026, buybacks ran at about $148.6M for the quarter, roughly $1.6M a day. The monthly core-contributor unlock on October 6, 2026 is about 9.92M HYPE, or roughly $0.86B at $87 to $90.

~$1.6Mbuyback spend per day at the Q2 2026 pace ($148.6M / 91 days)
~$0.86Bvalue of the Oct 6 unlock (9.92M HYPE)
~17 monthsof buybacks to match it ($860M / $1.6M a day = about 527 days)

That sounds alarming, and it is the right sizing question, but context matters: actual claims against the scheduled unlock have been only about 1.4% to 17.6% of the scheduled amount in recent months. Whether insiders sell, stake or hold is what moves the price, not the schedule on paper. [34][41]

The engine is volume-dependent, and volume has cooled from its peak. Quarterly gross protocol revenue fell from about $357M in Q3 2025 to about $202M in Q2 2026, a 43% drop, even as the token set record highs, and the share of revenue paid out to builders and market deployers rose from under 6% to about 18%. [34]

Two things partly offset the volume dependence. First, in June 2026 validators passed AQA v2, which routes about 90% of the yield on roughly $5B of USDC reserves to buybacks (an estimated $135M to $182M a year), with accrual from August 26 and a first payment around October 3: income that does not depend on trading volume. Second, token buybacks across the whole crypto industry hit a record $638M by the end of August 2026, and Hyperliquid plus one other protocol made up about 90% of it. Some analysts now describe this as a buyback yield of roughly 3.9%: the share of HYPE's market value retired each year. [35][24]

On DefiLlama's definitions, Hyperliquid's fees were $620M so far in 2026 (to October 3) against $972M for all of 2025, and protocol-retained revenue was $465M against $818M. [32]

QWhat are the risks and rewards of PURR, and what does MicroStrategy teach us?

Hyperliquid Strategies (Nasdaq: PURR) is, in a Coin Bureau analyst's words, "the MicroStrategy playbook pointed straight at HYPE". It began as a reverse merger in December 2025, when a former biotech shell, Sonnet BioTherapeutics, was repurposed as a HYPE treasury vehicle chaired by Bob Diamond, the former CEO of Barclays. [23]

The reflexivity problem: the same knife cuts both ways

PURR reported net income of $152.5 million for one quarter, almost entirely unrealized mark-to-market gains on HYPE. Over the full nine months to March 31, 2026 it reported a net loss of $165.4 million. Same holding, same accounting; the sign depends on the window. [23]

Worked example: how much one bad week can swing the holdings

On roughly 33 to 35M HYPE, a $10 move in the token changes the value of PURR's holdings by:

33,000,000 to 35,000,000 x $10 = $330M to $350M

A single move that size is larger than the company's whole reported net income in most periods, which is the defining feature (and danger) of a mark-to-market treasury vehicle.

The MSTR lessons that transfer directly:

  • The premium to NAV is the whole game. Treasury stocks trade at premiums and discounts to their holdings; you are betting on that premium as well as the asset.
  • Reflexivity cuts identically in both directions. Leverage and capital raises that inflate the gains also accelerate the losses.
  • Index inclusion manufactures demand that is not conviction. Passive funds must buy regardless of valuation.
  • Concentration is the point and the risk. One token's price is the whole thesis.

It is a growing category, not a one-off: billionaire Stanley Druckenmiller's family office disclosed a new $23 million stake in a 13F filing, and Hyperion DeFi (Nasdaq: HYPD) held about 2.04M HYPE at June 30. Disclosure: Pantera, whose research is cited here, is an anchor investor in PURR. [24][43][29]

QWhy does Bitwise CIO Matt Hougan's position on HYPE and BHYP matter?

Bitwise's Chief Investment Officer has staked out one of the most aggressive institutional theses: framing Hyperliquid not as another DEX token but as a "super app" targeting the $600 trillion global asset market, the combined value of equities, bonds, commodities and currencies that RWA perps could eventually bring on-chain. [23]

  • Why it carries weight: it is the thesis behind a regulated, SEC-registered product (BHYP) from a major asset manager, not a retail influencer take.
  • It is backed by product action. BHYP launched May 15, 2026, and a Bitwise analyst called the launch cohort "the best single-asset crypto ETP launch since Bitcoin itself".
  • It reframes valuation. If you accept the super-app thesis, HYPE is priced against a sliver of global market infrastructure, not against other Layer-1 tokens. Multicoin's 2028 scenarios (about $109 bear, $319 base, $689 bull per token, written when HYPE was near $63) show how wide that range is. [28]

Weigh the source, not just the size

Bitwise's BHYP benefits financially from HYPE adoption and inflows. That does not make the thesis wrong, but it is a direct financial interest, not disinterested research. The same applies to Pantera and Multicoin, which both disclose holdings.

A practical watchlist

  • Track quarterly protocol revenue and buybacks above almost everything else; they are the earliest tell that the flywheel is slowing.
  • Watch how each unlock and OTC sale actually behaves in the market, not just its size on paper.
  • Watch PURR's premium to the value of its HYPE. When it compresses, the leverage works downward.
  • Treat any technical reading (an RSI near 77 was noted in June) as dated; check momentum yourself.
  • If you hold exposure through an ETF, weigh the added custodial and structural layer against the convenience.

QWhat are the actual steps to place a trade?

  1. Confirm eligibility and complete derivatives onboarding

    Spot KYC does not automatically cover derivatives. Coinbase and Kraken each require a separate derivatives agreement, risk disclosures, and sometimes a suitability or eligibility check before the perp account activates.

  2. Fund the derivatives account

    Transfer USD or approved collateral into the derivatives wallet; margin usually cannot be pulled automatically from a spot balance mid-trade.

  3. Read the actual contract spec before the marketing name

    Check tick size, contract size, the index the settlement price is drawn from, the funding interval, and whether it is a true perpetual or a dated "perpetual-style" wrapper.

  4. Size the position and choose leverage

    Decide direction, notional size and leverage before looking at the order ticket, not while watching the price move.

  5. Set the exit before the entry

    Place a stop-loss and take-profit, and know the liquidation price, before the order fills.

  6. Submit the order

    Use a market order for an immediate fill or a limit order to control the entry price. Confirm margin and maintenance-margin ratio once it is open.

  7. Track funding

    Perps bill or credit funding every few hours (Fig. 1). On a small account, funding drag can matter as much as price movement over a multi-day hold.

  8. Close and reconcile

    Exit with an opposite order or let the stop or target trigger, then reconcile realized P&L. See the tax note below.

QWhat should stay on the permanent watchlist?

Market and execution risk

  • Liquidation cascades in thin, off-peak liquidity: size down into low-volume hours.
  • Funding-rate flips: a crowded trade can flip from paying you to costing you overnight.
  • A simple sizing floor: risk no more than 1% to 2% of account equity on any single trade, regardless of how much leverage the venue allows.

Legal and regulatory items to track

  • CME and ICE versus the CFTC and Hyperliquid: still unresolved as of October 2026; a ruling or rule change could alter the onshore-perpetual approvals. [12][13]
  • Contract structure is not the marketing name. Coinbase's product is a 5-year dated future marketed as "perpetual-style"; Kraken's is a true no-expiry perpetual. Read the contract spec.
  • Tax treatment. Futures on a CFTC-regulated exchange are typically Section 1256 contracts: gains get 60% long-term and 40% short-term treatment and are marked to market at year-end regardless of holding period. That differs from spot crypto; confirm current-year treatment with a tax professional, since it depends on the venue's regulatory status.
  • Offshore trading as a US person (for example via a VPN to Hyperliquid, or through a Hyperliquid-routed wallet) sits outside this protection and carries its own legal and reporting risk. It is not a shortcut around the venues above.
  • Check cftc.gov and nfa.futures.org directly for current rule status rather than relying on any single article, including this one.

QHow do builder codes work, and what do they earn?

Builder codes are the distribution business model. Any app can attach its own fee to the orders it routes to Hyperliquid, and the fee is credited to the app's address, with no matching engine, custody or clearing to build. The mechanics, verified against Hyperliquid's docs on October 3, 2026: [57][58]

  1. The user approves a maximum fee, once

    The user's main wallet signs an approveBuilderFee action naming the builder address and a maximum rate (for example "0.05%"). An agent wallet cannot do this, so the approval screen is where your app earns trust: show the exact builder and fee.

  2. The app attaches the builder code to each order

    The order action carries "builder": {"b": "<address>", "f": <int>}, where f is in tenths of a basis point (so f = 10 is 1 bp). Each order's fee cannot exceed what the user approved.

  3. The fee accrues to the builder

    It is charged on top of Hyperliquid's own fee and tracked in the referral-reward system; builders claim it from the Referrals page. Builder fill files and the Info API support reconciliation.

0.1%maximum builder fee on perps (f = 100)
1%maximum on spot, charged on the selling side
10active builder approvals allowed per user
100 USDCminimum perps account value for the builder address

QStep by step: how do you ship an application?

The lighter path first, since it is realistic for almost any team. Facts below were checked against Hyperliquid's developer docs and the official SDK on October 3, 2026; items I could not confirm are flagged. [58]

  1. Pick a lane

    Start by building against existing markets via the API. Reserve a HIP-3 deployment for when you have an asset class only you can bring.

  2. Set up the SDK

    Official Python SDK (pip install hyperliquid-python-sdk); community TypeScript SDK @nktkas/hyperliquid; an official Rust SDK also exists (verify builder-fee support before relying on it). The API is POST /info (public, read-only), POST /exchange (signed writes) and a WebSocket at wss://api.hyperliquid.xyz/ws.

  3. Prototype read-only on testnet

    Testnet REST is api.hyperliquid-testnet.xyz; the faucet gives 1,000 mock USDC (the address must have deposited on mainnet). The public Info API needs no authentication; see the examples below.

  4. Use an agent wallet for trading

    The main wallet signs an approveAgent action once; the agent key then signs orders. Limits: one unnamed plus three named agents per account, expiry of up to 180 days. Agents cannot approve builder fees or transfer funds.

  5. Approve the builder fee and trade with a builder code

    See the official SDK example below. Show the user the exact builder address and maximum fee before they sign.

  6. Handle the unglamorous parts

    Prices: at most 5 significant figures and the asset's decimal limits; sizes rounded to the asset's size decimals. Nonces: millisecond timestamps from an atomic counter, unique per signer; use one agent key per process. Rate limits: REST weight 1,200 per minute per IP, WebSocket 10 connections per IP; handle reconnects.

  7. Optional: add HyperEVM contracts

    Mainnet chain ID 999, RPC https://rpc.hyperliquid.xyz/evm, gas token HYPE. Contracts read HyperCore state through precompiles and write through CoreWriter at 0x3333…3333 (actions are delayed a few seconds on Core). The full precompile address map was not on the pages checked, so confirm addresses in the docs. [59]

  8. Ambitious path: deploy a HIP-3 market

    Stake 500,000 HYPE (at least 183 days), define the market and oracle, supply prices and risk limits, and earn up to 50% of fees. The first three assets need no auction; more go through a shared Dutch auction. HIP-4 deployment steps are not yet documented, so plan as a consumer using builder codes. [21]

  9. Ship, monitor and get a security review

    Keep keys short-lived and out of client code, use a KMS or HSM for backend keys, monitor builder fills for reconciliation, and run an independent review before mainnet.

Injective

The strongest finance-native competitor to Hyperliquid's approach — a Layer-1 built around an on-chain order book from day one, with its own take on RWA perpetuals.

QWhat is Injective?

Injective is a Layer-1 blockchain built on the Cosmos SDK, purpose-built for finance rather than general-purpose smart contracts. Where Hyperliquid is "an exchange with an L1 wrapped around it," Injective is closer to the inverse: a general-purpose finance L1 that ships a fully decentralized order book as a first-class, chain-level module rather than a smart contract sitting on top. [63][64]

Three design choices define it:

  • A native on-chain order book, not an AMM. Injective's Exchange module handles order matching, spot, and derivatives directly at the chain level, using batch auctions to settle orders — a mechanism specifically meant to neutralize the front-running/MEV that plagues sequential, mempool-based matching. [63]
  • Chain-level shared infrastructure. Per CoinGecko's own framing, oracle pricing, orderbook management, matching, margin calculations, liquidation, and even the insurance fund all run as shared network services rather than something every application has to rebuild — so a new market or app plugs into the same canonical infrastructure instead of fragmenting liquidity across siloed venues. [62]
  • MultiVM, one shared liquidity layer. Injective's original environment is CosmWasm (WASM); an EVM mainnet — letting a standard Solidity/Hardhat stack deploy directly — launched November 11, 2025; an SVM (Solana-program) track has also been announced. All settle against the same native USDC and the same canonical order books, rather than fragmenting across bridges. [65]

Tokenomics: INJ has a fixed 100M max supply and a weekly Burn Auction — a basket of protocol fees collected across the ecosystem is auctioned off, and the winning bid (paid in INJ) is destroyed permanently, directly linking real usage to token scarcity. On the regulatory side, INJ itself began trading as a CFTC-regulated future via Bitnomial on April 15, 2026 — the same "license through an existing US derivatives entity" pattern behind Kraken's onshore crypto perps in Section 1. [66][63]

QWhat does Injective do in RWA-perps specifically?

Injective's RWA product is built on its iAssets framework — described by CoinGecko as "programmable primitives built for position-based exposure and cross-market composability." The point is architectural: iAssets let applications route orders, monitor margin, automate strategies, and combine exposures without requiring the underlying stock, commodity, or currency to be wrapped, custodied, or pre-funded on the network — exposure through position, not through holding a tokenized copy of the asset.
