01

Perpetuals, plainly

A perpetual future ("perp") is the single instrument behind most of the leverage in crypto. Before touching Hyperliquid or any US venue, get this mechanism solid — it's the same math everywhere it shows up.

QWhat is perpetual futures trading, and why is it such a big deal?

A regular futures contract locks in a price for an asset on a set expiry date — like a forward-dated invoice. A perpetual future has no expiry. You can hold it for an hour or three years. It behaves like leveraged spot trading, except you never actually own the coin, stock, or barrel of oil — you own a contract whose value tracks it.

The trick is keeping a contract with no expiry date glued to the real ("spot") price of the asset, since there's no expiry date to force convergence. Exchanges solve this with the funding rate: a small periodic payment between longs (buyers) and shorts (sellers).

Why does this matter so much? Three reasons:

  • Leverage without borrowing. Post $1,000 as margin, control a $10,000 position — no loan paperwork, no collateral custody at a lender. The exchange nets your margin against your open risk in real time.
  • It's the dominant way the world actually trades crypto. Perpetuals now account for the large majority of all crypto trading volume — more than spot trading, more than dated futures — because they never force you to "roll" a position at expiry.
  • It went from a crypto curiosity to a systemic financial story in 2026. Regulators, CME, Kraken, Coinbase, Kalshi, the White House, and Hyperliquid are now openly fighting over who gets to host this trillion-dollar-a-year flow — see Sections 2 and 3.

Worked example — a $1,000 BTC perp trade

BTC spot is $110,000. You open a long with $1,000 margin at 10× leverage.

$10,000Notional position size (margin × leverage)
0.0909 BTCContract exposure ($10,000 ÷ $110,000)
≈10%Maintenance margin buffer before liquidation (varies by venue)

BTC rises 5% to $115,500 → your $10,000 notional gains $500. Against your $1,000 margin, that's a +50% account return — the leverage cuts both ways. A 5% move against you erases half your margin; a ~10% adverse move at 10× leverage gets you close to liquidation, where the exchange force-closes the position before your losses exceed your posted margin.

QWhy has this become such a big deal, specifically now?

Three forces converged in 2026:

  1. Scale. On-chain perpetual exchanges alone processed roughly $423 billion in 30 days across the sector, and the category (on-chain + offshore centralized) is estimated to represent the majority of all crypto trading turnover. [1]
  2. The US spent years locked out. Because the CFTC treats leveraged, margined, no-delivery crypto contracts as regulated derivatives, every major perp venue (Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid) has geo-blocked US persons — pushing an enormous, liquid market entirely offshore, out of reach of US retail and out of the US tax and compliance net.
  3. That wall started coming down in 2026. A joint SEC/CFTC digital-asset taxonomy (March 17, 2026) gave the CFTC clear jurisdiction to license onshore perpetual-style products, and Kraken, Coinbase, and Kalshi all launched CFTC-regulated versions within months of each other. [2][3]

Hyperliquid

The single platform most responsible for turning "perpetuals" from a crypto-native niche into a mainstream financial story in 2026.

QWhat is Hyperliquid?

Hyperliquid is its own purpose-built Layer-1 blockchain, designed from scratch to run a fully on-chain order book — the same style of matching engine a stock exchange or Binance uses — instead of the slower automated-market-maker pools most DeFi exchanges rely on. It has two halves:

  • HyperCore — the trading engine itself: the order book, matching, margining, and liquidation logic, running on-chain at speeds that feel like a centralized exchange.
  • HyperEVM — a fully Solidity-compatible smart-contract layer sitting next to HyperCore, byte-compatible with the Ethereum Virtual Machine, so your existing Hardhat/Foundry toolchain works on it unmodified. Contracts here can read and act on live HyperCore data — balances, positions, oracle prices, vault state.

It's non-custodial (you connect a wallet, no account or KYC to trade), lists 100+ perpetual markets, and — notably for your roadmap — has begun listing non-crypto instruments too (its HIP-3 framework let a builder list 24/7 oil perpetuals, for example). Section 4 goes deep on exactly how this works under the hood; Section 7 looks at a very different kind of "RWA" story — 1inch's spot-aggregated access to tokenized assets, with no leverage at all.

Lane B — Trade (currently blocked for US persons)

QWhat are the actual steps to place a trade?

  1. Confirm eligibility & complete derivatives onboarding

    Your existing spot KYC does not automatically cover derivatives. Kraken and Coinbase each require a separate derivatives agreement, risk disclosures, and sometimes a suitability questionnaire before the perp account activates.

  2. Fund the derivatives sub-account

    Transfer USD (or approved collateral) from spot into the specific derivatives wallet — margin usually cannot be pulled automatically from your spot balance mid-trade.

  3. Read the actual contract spec before the marketing name

    Check tick size, contract size, the exact index/oracle the settlement price is drawn from, and — critically — whether it's a true perpetual or a dated "perpetual-style" wrapper (see the Section 3.1 table).

  4. Size the position and choose leverage

    Decide direction (long/short), notional size, and leverage before looking at the order ticket — not while watching the price move.

  5. Set your exit before your entry

    Place a stop-loss and take-profit, and calculate your liquidation price, before the order fills — not after.

  6. Submit the order

    Market order for immediate fill, limit order to control your entry price. Confirm margin and maintenance-margin ratio on the position screen once it's open.

  7. Track funding payments

    Perpetuals bill or credit funding every few hours (Fig. 1). On a small account, funding drag can matter as much as price movement over a multi-day hold.

  8. Close and reconcile

    Exit with an opposite order or let your stop/target trigger, then reconcile realized P&L — see the tax note below.

Worked example — a regulated perp trade end to end

You go long 1 BTC-equivalent notional at $110,000 using 5× leverage.

$22,000Margin required (notional ÷ leverage)
+$5,500Unrealized gain if BTC rises 5% to $115,500
+25%That gain as a return on your $22,000 margin

With a typical 3% maintenance-margin requirement, your approximate liquidation price sits near $110,000 × (1 − 20% + 3%) ≈ $93,500 — roughly a 15% adverse move at 5× leverage. Funding, at a representative rate of 0.01% per 8 hours paid by longs when the perp trades above spot, would cost this position about $11 per 8-hour period ($10,000 notional-adjusted) — small per period, but it compounds on a position held for weeks.

QWhat should stay on my permanent watchlist?

Market & execution risk

  • Liquidation cascades in thin, off-peak liquidity — size down going into low-volume hours.
  • Funding-rate flips: a crowded trade can flip from paying you to costing you overnight.
  • A simple position-sizing floor: risk no more than 1–2% of account equity on any single trade, independent of how much leverage the venue allows.

Legal & regulatory items to track

  • CME v. CFTC litigation — still unresolved as of Sep 2026; a ruling could alter or unwind the current onshore-perpetual approvals for Kraken and Kalshi. [6]
  • Contract structure ≠ marketing name — Coinbase's product is a dated 5-year "nano" future marketed as "perpetual-style"; Kraken's (via Bitnomial) is a true no-expiry perpetual. Read the actual contract spec.
  • Tax treatment — futures on a CFTC-regulated exchange are typically Section 1256 contracts: gains get 60% long-term / 40% short-term capital-gains treatment and are marked-to-market at year-end, regardless of how long you held them. That's a meaningfully different (often better) tax profile than spot crypto — confirm current-year treatment with a tax professional, since this hinges on the exchange's regulatory status.
  • Offshore trading as a US person (e.g., via VPN to Hyperliquid or Binance) sits outside this protection entirely and carries its own reporting and legal risk — it is not a shortcut around the venues above.
  • Check cftc.gov and nfa.futures.org directly for the current rule status rather than relying on any single article, including this one.

Architecture & the RWA thesis

Why serious analysts — a16z crypto among them — treat Hyperliquid as the leading on-chain candidate to host real-world-asset (RWA) trading, not just crypto perps.

QHow does HyperCore actually achieve exchange-grade speed on-chain?

Most blockchains process transactions one after another and settle in seconds at best — fine for a token swap, unworkable for a limit order book that needs to match, cancel, and liquidate continuously. HyperCore is built differently, purpose-first:

LayerWhat it doesWhy it matters for RWA
HyperBFT consensusA HotStuff-derived Byzantine-fault-tolerant protocol with a rotating leader and pipelined rounds — proposal, voting, and commitment overlap instead of running in sequence, tolerating up to 1/3 malicious validators. [19]Median latency of ~0.1s, 99th-percentile ~0.5s, one-block finality — fast enough that a stock or commodity trader wouldn't notice they're on-chain. [19]
HyperCore matching engineA Rust-based virtual machine purpose-built for trading logic — order matching runs as a native operation, not an expensive general-purpose smart-contract call. [19]Throughput up to ~200,000 orders/second, the difference between a toy on-chain order book and one that can absorb real institutional RWA flow. [19]
HyperEVMAn EVM-compatible execution layer next to HyperCore, able to read and act on live trading state (prices, positions, oracle feeds) from Solidity contracts. [16]Lets a Hardhat/Foundry team build structured products, vaults, or AI-driven strategies directly against real RWA price data, without leaving the chain.

QWhat exactly is HIP-3, and how does it turn Hyperliquid into an open market-listing venue?

HIP-3 ("builder-deployed perpetuals") lets any qualifying team launch its own perpetual market directly on HyperCore — without building a separate exchange, matching engine, or liquidity network. It went live on mainnet October 13, 2025. [20]

500,000 HYPESecurity bond a deployer must stake (≈$25M at listing-era prices)
50%Share of trading fees the deployer earns on their own markets
3 freeMarkets any deployer can launch before needing a Dutch auction for more
100%Of the stake validators can slash for malicious oracle/market conduct

A deployer configures the asset, oracle source and fallback logic, tick size, margin model, leverage caps, and open-interest caps — then trades route through the exact same HyperCore order path as native BTC/ETH markets. That shared execution layer is the key design decision: a HIP-3 market doesn't bootstrap its own liquidity from zero — it plugs into infrastructure that's already fast and already has traders on it. Malicious behavior (bad oracle feeds, manipulated settlement) is checked by a stake-weighted validator vote that can destroy the deployer's bond, with a 7-day unstaking window to prevent an exit-scam. [20][21]

QAnd HIP-4?

HIP-4 ("outcome trading") is Hyperliquid's prediction-market primitive — fully collateralized, expiry-based contracts that settle to exactly 0 or 1 based on a real-world event, via an authorized oracle. It reached mainnet on May 2, 2026, with Outcomexyz as the first marketplace (initially daily Bitcoin price predictions). [22]

  • No liquidation risk — contracts are fully collateralized, so the most you can lose is your stake, unlike a leveraged perp.
  • Merged YES/NO order books, USDH collateral, and zero fees to open a position.
  • Deployment is staged: Phase 1 kept markets curated by the Hyperliquid team; since August 29, 2026, builders staking 500,000 HYPE can deploy from validator-approved templates, with a fully permissionless Phase 2 gated at 1,000,000 HYPE. [22]
  • The composability payoff: because HIP-4 lives on the same HyperCore account as your perps, you can hold a directional perpetual position and an event-based hedge in one margin account — something siloed venues like Polymarket or Kalshi simply can't offer, since they aren't also your perpetuals broker.

No expiry, no physical delivery, no transfer agent, no dividends, no voting rights — trade[XYZ] deliberately stayed inside the derivative wrapper HIP-3 already provides rather than trying to tokenize real equity, which would pull in securities law it doesn't need to touch. [26]
  • The front-end doesn't custody anything. trade[XYZ]'s own docs describe it as a non-custodial interface that doesn't hold user assets or operate HyperCore itself — its markets are explicitly "not the exclusive way" to access them, since anyone can route to the same HIP-3 markets through builder codes or their own UI. [28]

The other reference point worth knowing: Outcomexyz, the first HIP-4 deployer, applies the identical pattern to prediction markets rather than perpetuals — a thin front-end over a shared, protocol-level primitive.

QStep by step — how do you actually ship an application on Hyperliquid?

The lighter path first, since it's realistic for almost any team to start here:

  1. Pick your lane: front-end/bot, or your own market

    Most teams should start by building against existing HIP-3/HIP-4 markets via the API — reserve a full HIP-3 deployment for when you have a specific asset class only you can bring (see Step 6 onward).

  2. Set up the SDK

    Hyperliquid ships official/community SDKs in Python, TypeScript, and Rust. The API is two POST endpoints — /info (public, read-only market data) and /exchange (signed order placement) — plus a WebSocket feed for live order-book and account updates. [29]

  3. Prototype against the Info API

    Pull order books, candles, funding rates, and open interest with no authentication needed — enough to build and backtest a strategy or dashboard before touching real capital.

  4. Activate an agent wallet

    Rather than signing every order with your main key, Hyperliquid supports temporary session keys ("agents") scoped to trading — deposit USDC, activate the agent, and it can place/cancel orders on your behalf until revoked.

  5. Register a builder code

    Attach your builder code to every order your users place through your app; you earn a share of the fees that flow generates, funding your app without custodying user assets.

  6. (Ambitious path) Define your market thesis

    If you're going further and deploying your own venue like trade[XYZ] did: decide which assets you're uniquely positioned to list — this is where your roadmap's pre-IPO and custom-index ambitions fit.

  7. Stake your HYPE bond

    Acquire and stake 500,000 HYPE (HIP-3) or the applicable HIP-4 threshold as your security bond — this is real capital at risk, slashable for misconduct, not a code deployment fee.

  8. Design oracle sourcing and fallback logic

    As deployer, you're responsible for accurate mark prices. For RWA specifically, this typically means aggregating a licensed market-data feed with at least one independent fallback source and a clear escalation path if a feed goes stale.

  9. Configure the market and launch

    Set tick size, margin model, leverage caps, and open-interest caps. Your first three markets launch free; additional assets go through the shared Dutch-auction process.

  10. Ship a non-custodial front-end and attract market makers

    Build the UI against the same shared HyperCore order path everyone else uses — you're not building a matching engine — then actively court liquidity providers, since thin books at launch are the main early-stage risk to your own markets.

Before you commit capital

A 500,000 HYPE bond is fully slashable — up to 100% — if validators vote that your market was operated maliciously (e.g., a manipulated or unmaintained oracle feed). Budget for monitoring and alerting on your own feed health as a first-class engineering cost, not an afterthought.

1inch

A very different kind of project from everything else in this document: no perpetuals, no leverage, no order book of its own — just the largest trade-routing layer in DeFi, and a live case study in whether volume alone makes a business.

QWhat is 1inch?

1inch is a DEX aggregator and intent-routing layer — it holds no liquidity and runs no order book of its own. Instead, its Pathfinder engine scans the entire on-chain liquidity graph — more than 350 individual liquidity sources (Uniswap, Curve, Balancer, and dozens more) across 13+ blockchains — and splits a single trade across multiple venues and intermediate tokens to find a better net price than any one DEX could offer alone. Founded in 2019 (originally a hackathon project by Sergej Kunz and Anton Bukov), it's now governed by the 1inch DAO, with the 1INCH token used for governance and as resolver collateral. [31][32]

350+Liquidity sources scanned by Pathfinder across 13+ chains
$0 gasOn Fusion mode — resolvers fill your signed intent instead of you paying gas
No bridgeFusion+ lets you swap, e.g., ETH on Ethereum for SOL on Solana with one signature

Three execution modes matter:

  • Classic — on-chain aggregation via Pathfinder; you pay gas, the router finds the best split.
  • Fusion — intent-based: you sign what you want, professional "resolvers" compete to fill it, you pay no gas, and MEV bots can't sandwich your order. Resolvers earn a small spread (a few basis points) baked into your quoted price.
  • Fusion+ — extends the same resolver model cross-chain, replacing bridges with a single signed intent.

The profitability paradox — what the referenced article is actually about

The CryptoBriefing report supplied for this edition lays out a striking gap between scale and business health. Since its 2019 launch, 1inch has routed roughly $814 billion in cumulative volume — $214 billion in 2025 alone (+39% YoY, across 114 million transactions). And yet co-founder Sergej Kunz says the business still isn't profitable, for a structural reason: as an aggregator, 1inch's own fees have to stay at or below the fees of the DEXs it routes through — charge more, and users (or the wallets embedding 1inch) simply route around it. The 1INCH token trades roughly 99% below its 2021 all-time high, with a market cap of only $100–130 million despite that volume. [33]

The lesson generalizes well beyond 1inch: routing enormous volume is not the same as capturing durable value. Compare this to Hyperliquid's fee-buyback model or a HIP-3 deployer's 50% fee share (Section 4) — both are designed specifically to capture value at the base matching/settlement layer, rather than as a thin routing layer sitting on top of someone else's liquidity, which is exactly the trap 1inch's numbers illustrate.

QWhat does 1inch actually do in RWA — and why not perps?

1inch has no native perpetual-futures product and offers no leverage anywhere in its stack — worth stating plainly, since every other project in this document is a perp venue. Its RWA story is a spot-swap story instead: in September 2025, 1inch partnered with Ondo Finance — one of the largest RWA platforms in crypto, known for tokenized US Treasury products like OUSG and USDY — to route access to tokenized real-world assets through 1inch's own Swap API. [34]

  • What it solves: the integration targets RWA's biggest adoption blocker — fragmented liquidity — by letting any wallet or dApp embedding 1inch's API offer tokenized-Treasury or tokenized-stock swaps with the same aggregated best-execution routing as any other token swap. Bitget and Trust Wallet are among the API partners already live with it. [34]
  • How it executes: RWA swaps route through the same resolver network as Fusion — professional market makers filling signed intents — not a perpetual order book, so there's no funding rate, no liquidation, and no leverage. You end up holding the actual tokenized instrument (or a claim on it), at spot, not a synthetic derivative position.
  • Scale: tokenized stock and ETF volume routed through the Ondo integration passed $2.5 billion cumulatively within its first months live; the CryptoBriefing article's slightly later figure of $3 billion in cumulative tokenized volume by March 2026 reflects the same growth a bit further along. [33][34]

How this differs from every other RWA product in this document

Sections 2–6 are about leveraged, synthetic RWA exposure — perpetual contracts on Hyperliquid that track a stock or commodity's price without ever touching the real asset. 1inch's RWA integration is the opposite: unleveraged, aggregated access to the actual tokenized instrument at the best available spot price. If your roadmap calls for leveraged directional or hedged exposure, 1inch is not the venue. If it calls for efficiently swapping into a tokenized Treasury or equity product at good execution, this is a legitimate — and very different — way to do it.
